clean semantics

all curator actions history

multi-step lifecycle trail
That’s where it becomes a workflow system.

ADR: Separate Resolution Decision Store from System of Records
Context and Problem Statement
The ER System currently distinguishes a Request Registry (System of Records) that preserves inbound resolution requests and their provenance (what was submitted, by whom, when, and with what entity mention representation). As the solution evolves to support human-in-the-loop curation, the system must also persist resolution decisions (including uncertain/pending decisions) and curator actions (accept, reject, assign, re-resolve) with a clear lifecycle and audit trail.

Storing decisions and curator actions inside the System of Records risks turning it into a catch‑all store with conflicting semantics and access patterns:

inbound requests are primarily append-only provenance artefacts,

decisions/actions require workflow state, filtering, assignment, and status transitions,

canonical identity state belongs to the Canonical Entity Registry and must remain distinct from decision history.

We need a design that supports curation workflows, traceability, and rebuild semantics, while keeping ownership and meaning of stores unambiguous.

Decision Drivers
Glossary alignment: “Resolution Decision” and “Curation Action” are first-class business concepts.

Single source of truth: Canonical identifiers and membership remain authoritative in the Canonical Entity Registry.

Operational simplicity: Avoid unnecessary store proliferation, but prevent semantic overload of the System of Records.

Auditability and explainability: Preserve decision lineage and curator interventions.

Replaceability: Keep the ERE pluggable; persistence of decisions remains within ERS.

Considered Options
Keep decisions and curator actions in the Request Registry (System of Records)

Introduce a dedicated Resolution Decision Store (pending + final decisions + curator actions)

Adopt event sourcing with an append-only Decision Event Log as primary store, and projections for worklists/state

Decision Outcome
Chosen option: 2 — Introduce a dedicated Resolution Decision Store.

What this means
The Request Registry (System of Records) stores inbound requests and mention context (provenance and reproducibility), and does not become the home for decision lifecycle management.

A new Resolution Decision Store stores:

system-generated resolution decisions (including uncertain / pending),

curator actions (accept/reject/assign/re-resolve),

decision status lifecycle (e.g., pending → reviewed → superseded),

references to entity mentions and canonical identifiers.

The Canonical Entity Registry remains authoritative for current identity state (canonical identifiers and membership), updated as a consequence of accepted decisions and curator actions.

Consequences
Positive
Clear separation of meanings: provenance vs decision lifecycle vs identity state.

Curation worklist becomes straightforward (filtering, assignment, SLA, status) without overloading the System of Records.

Stronger auditability: curator actions are first-class records.

Better rebuild story: decisions can be replayed or re-evaluated without conflating with inbound requests.

Canonical Entity Registry remains optimised for current identity lookup.

Negative
One additional store to operate, monitor, and back up.

Requires explicit consistency rules between Decision Store and Canonical Registry (eventual consistency or idempotent updates).

Neutral / follow-ups
The Canonical Entity Registry MAY keep a lightweight reference to decision lifecycle (e.g. latestDecisionId, needsCuration flag), but MUST NOT store full decision history or workflow state.

If future requirements demand strict replayability across all decision changes, we may evolve toward event sourcing (Option 3), using the Decision Store as a stepping stone.

Confirmation
We will consider the decision successful if:

The curation application can produce the pending decision queue solely from the Resolution Decision Store.

Each curator action is traceable to a specific Resolution Decision and leads to a well-defined status transition.

The Canonical Entity Registry reflects the latest accepted state and can be reconciled against decisions.

The System of Records remains limited to inbound requests and mention context, without decision workflow logic.
